## What is Tow Ball Mass?

**Tow Ball Mass (TBM)** — sometimes called **towball download** — is the vertical force that the caravan's coupling applies to the tow vehicle's towball when the caravan is hitched.

TBM is part of the caravan's ATM but is borne by the tow vehicle rather than the trailer's axles.

## The Ideal TBM Range

For good towing stability, TBM should be **8–12% of ATM**.

| Caravan ATM | Ideal TBM range |
|-------------|-----------------|
| 2,000 kg | 160–240 kg |
| 2,500 kg | 200–300 kg |
| 3,000 kg | 240–360 kg |
| 3,500 kg | 280–420 kg |

<Callout type="warning">
Too little TBM (under ~8%) causes caravan **sway** — the van fishtails at speed and can be extremely dangerous. Too much TBM reduces front axle load on your vehicle, degrading steering and braking.
</Callout>

## Why TBM Eats Into Your GVM

TBM is added to your vehicle's loaded weight. This means it directly reduces how much [GVM headroom](/guides/gvm-explained/) you have available.

```
Vehicle GVM headroom required ≥ TBM
```

This is one of the most common reasons a legally rated tow combination still exceeds the vehicle's GVM.

## How to Measure TBM

The most accurate way is a **towball scale** (also called a towball download gauge). You can purchase one or borrow one from a caravan club.

Some weighbridges offer a split weighing service that separates front and rear axle weights.

## TBM and Weight Distribution Hitches

For heavy rigs, a **weight distribution hitch (WDH)** transfers some towball download back to the tow vehicle's front axle and to the trailer's axles. This improves level and steering, but the TBM still applies to the towball from a mass perspective.

## What is GVM?

**Gross Vehicle Mass (GVM)** is the maximum total mass your vehicle is legally permitted to carry on public roads. It is set by the manufacturer and stamped on the vehicle's compliance plate.

GVM includes:

- The kerb weight of the vehicle (fuel, fluids, spare tyre)
- All passengers and their luggage
- Any aftermarket accessories fitted to the vehicle (bull bar, tray, canopy, fridge, etc.)
- The **towball download** from your caravan or trailer

<Callout type="warning">
Many owners discover their loaded vehicle already exceeds GVM **before** hitching a trailer. Accessories like bull bars, roof racks, and canopies add up faster than most people expect.
</Callout>

## GVM Headroom

**GVM headroom** is the difference between your vehicle's GVM and its actual loaded weight:

```
GVM headroom = GVM − loaded vehicle weight
```

This headroom is what the towball download must fit within. A caravan's **Tow Ball Mass (TBM)** is typically 8–12% of its ATM (Aggregate Trailer Mass).

### Example

| Item | Weight |
|------|--------|
| Kerb weight (Toyota LandCruiser 300 Series) | 2,440 kg |
| Occupants + luggage | 300 kg |
| Bull bar + recovery gear | 80 kg |
| Fridge, dual battery, accessories | 60 kg |
| **Loaded vehicle weight** | **2,880 kg** |
| **GVM** | **3,100 kg** |
| **Headroom for towball download** | **220 kg** |

## Why Exceeding GVM is Serious

Exceeding GVM is not just a fine — it can:

1. **Void your insurance** — most policies exclude cover when the vehicle is overloaded
2. **Shift legal liability** under Chain of Responsibility (CoR) laws
3. **Compromise braking and handling** — especially on long descents
4. **Damage suspension and tyres** prematurely

## GVM Upgrades

A GVM upgrade legally increases the maximum load rating of your vehicle. Upgrading must be performed by an accredited modifier and results in a new compliance plate.

Popular upgrades include suspension-based GVM lifts for dual-cab utes, commonly raising GVM from 3,200 kg to 3,500 kg or beyond.

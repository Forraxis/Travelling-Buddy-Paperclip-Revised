## Why Bull Bar Weight Matters for Towing

A bull bar is one of the heaviest aftermarket accessories you can fit, and almost all of its weight sits ahead of the front axle. This creates two problems:

1. **GVM consumption** — the bull bar's mass directly reduces your available payload and towball download headroom
2. **Front axle overload** — because the weight is far forward, the front axle bears a disproportionate share

<Callout type="warning">
Many tow vehicle owners fit a bull bar **before** calculating whether their loaded vehicle still fits within GVM. Run your numbers first.
</Callout>

## Typical Bull Bar Weights

| Bar type | Typical weight |
|----------|---------------|
| Steel full-width bar | 40–80 kg |
| Alloy full-width bar | 20–45 kg |
| Nudge bar (alloy) | 8–18 kg |
| Winch bar (steel, with winch) | 80–130 kg |

These figures are for the bar alone. Mounting brackets, driving lights, and aerials add more.

## Calculating the Impact on Your Setup

1. Find your vehicle's current loaded weight (kerb weight + passengers + gear)
2. Add the bull bar weight
3. Compare against your vehicle's GVM
4. Calculate remaining headroom for towball download

### Example

| Item | Weight |
|------|--------|
| Loaded vehicle (no accessories) | 2,700 kg |
| Steel bull bar + winch | 110 kg |
| **New loaded weight** | **2,810 kg** |
| GVM | 3,100 kg |
| **GVM headroom remaining** | **290 kg** |

With a caravan TBM of 280 kg (on a 2,800 kg ATM van), this setup would be right on the limit — even before adding any other accessories.

## Staying Within Limits

- Choose an **alloy bar** over steel where protection needs allow
- Offset bar weight by removing unused factory equipment (tow bar if not needed, spare wheel carrier, etc.)
- Consider a **GVM upgrade** if you need more headroom
